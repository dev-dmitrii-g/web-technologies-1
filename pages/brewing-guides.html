Brewing Guides

Learn the basics of preparing coffee at home with three popular methods: V60, French Press and AeroPress. These guides provide simple starting points for coffee, water, grind size and brewing time.

About Home Brewing

Home brewing gives you control over coffee strength, water, grind size and brewing time. Small changes can affect the final taste, so it is useful to change one part of the process at a time.

The ratios and instructions below are starting points rather than strict rules. Coffee beans, grinders, water and personal taste can all affect the result.

Before You Start

  • Use fresh coffee beans when possible.
  • Use clean water with a suitable temperature.
  • Measure coffee and water consistently.
  • Keep track of brewing time.
  • Change one variable at a time when experimenting.

V60

V60 is a pour-over method that uses a cone-shaped dripper and paper filter. Water is poured over ground coffee in controlled stages.

A good starting ratio is approximately 1 g coffee : 16-17 g water.

  1. Place the paper filter in the dripper.
  2. Rinse the filter with hot water.
  3. Add ground coffee.
  4. Bloom the coffee with a small amount of water.
  5. Pour the remaining water slowly and evenly.
  6. Allow the coffee to finish dripping.

Starting ratio: 1:16-17

French Press

French Press brewing uses immersion. Coffee grounds stay in contact with water before the filter is pressed down.

A useful starting ratio is approximately 1 g coffee : 15 g water.

  1. Add coarse-ground coffee to the French Press.
  2. Add hot water.
  3. Stir gently to wet the grounds.
  4. Wait for approximately four minutes.
  5. Press the filter down slowly.
  6. Serve the coffee immediately.

AeroPress

AeroPress is an immersion and pressure-based brewing method. It can produce a clean and concentrated cup in a relatively short time.

A simple starting ratio is approximately 1 g coffee : 15 g water.

  1. Place the filter in the filter cap.
  2. Rinse the filter with hot water.
  3. Add medium-fine ground coffee.
  4. Add hot water and stir.
  5. Allow the coffee to brew briefly.
  6. Press slowly into the cup.

Troubleshooting

Coffee tastes too bitter
Try a coarser grind, shorter brewing time or slightly cooler water.
Coffee tastes too sour
Try a finer grind, longer brewing time or slightly hotter water.
Coffee tastes weak
Check the coffee-to-water ratio and consider using more coffee.
Coffee tastes too strong
Try using more water or slightly less coffee.
